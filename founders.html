Why this

Software ships in seconds. Hardware ships next week.

Between us we've lived the hardware loop from both ends — lab work at an RF company on one side, award-winning additive design and fabrication on the other — and we've built enough software to know how the other loop feels. Software is tight: change it, build it, see it, fix it — minutes. Hardware isn't. You send a board to a fab, wait out the week, find the one mistake, and start the clock over.

That gap — between how fast you can think and how slowly you can hold the result in your hand — is the thing we want to close. PrometheusPCB is our answer: pull fabrication onto the bench, so the wait stops being the bottleneck and your ideas start being the bottleneck again.

Why now

The pieces finally line up.

Low-temperature co-fired ceramic and direct ink writing are both mature enough to build a real machine around — decades of production literature to stand on, with the open questions narrowed to the machine itself and one named materials problem the technical brief takes head-on. And ceramic doesn't just carry the traces; it carries the passives. LTCC has buried resistors, capacitors, and inductors inside its layers in production for decades — which is what lets a desktop machine fab most of a board's parts instead of just its wiring. Meanwhile the only tools doing true multi-layer additive electronics today are six-figure closed boxes, and everyone else is still mailing designs to a fab. There's a gap exactly where a desktop, open machine should be. And the wave arriving behind it makes the gap urgent: startups are pushing back into the physical world, and every drone, robot, sensor, and defense system they build needs working boards faster than a fab cycle can turn them.

Texas Tech's Innovation Hub and an NSF I-Corps grant gave us the room to start chasing that gap now, instead of someday. The technical brief lays out the architecture and the one materials problem the whole thing turns on.

What's next

PrometheusPCB is early and moving fast. With the paper research settled, we're heads-down building our first functional prototype and talking to the hardware teams and labs who'd use it.
